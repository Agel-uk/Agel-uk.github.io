Robot Vacuum Buying Guide

Robot vacuums can reduce routine floor cleaning, but performance depends heavily on your home layout. Navigation, obstacle handling and replacement-part availability often matter more over time than a single suction number.

Map your home first

Consider thresholds, rugs, cables, pet bowls, stairs and low furniture. A robot that performs well in an open apartment may struggle in a cluttered home. Measure furniture clearance if you expect the robot to clean underneath.

Navigation and obstacle avoidance

LiDAR and camera-based systems can improve mapping, but implementation varies. Look for reliable room division, no-go zones and recovery after getting stuck. Homes with pets or many small objects benefit from stronger obstacle detection.

Vacuuming and mopping

If you mainly need carpet cleaning, focus on brush design and carpet behavior. For hard floors, mopping can be useful, but basic drag pads are different from powered or lifting mop systems. Check whether the robot can avoid wetting carpets.

Maintenance cost

Brushes, filters, bags and mop pads are recurring costs. Before buying, check whether replacements are readily available in your market and whether the app and cloud service have a credible support history.

Common mistakes

Do not assume the highest suction figure guarantees the best cleaning. Navigation, brush contact, software, floor type and maintenance can have a larger effect on everyday results.
